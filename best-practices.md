# Best Practices — LinkedIn Content

## The Fundamentals

LinkedIn's algorithm and audience behavior reward specific patterns. These best practices are derived from platform mechanics, engagement data, and continuous observation — not generic social media advice.

---

## Post Structure

### The 3-Part Structure (Non-Negotiable)

```
HOOK        → Stops the scroll (1-2 lines)
BODY        → Delivers the value
CLOSER      → Drives action, reflection, or sharing
```

Every post must have all three. A post without a strong hook is invisible. A post without a strong closer leaves engagement on the table.

---

## The Hook

The hook is the only thing the algorithm shows before "…see more." It determines whether the post gets read at all.

**Rules:**
- Maximum 2 lines before the fold
- Must create curiosity, tension, surprise, or a bold claim
- Never start with "I" as the first word (weak)
- Never start with a compliment to someone else as the main hook
- The hook must connect directly to the body — no bait and switch

**Hook structure options:**
1. **One powerful sentence** — "Your LinkedIn strategy is making you invisible."
2. **Short + follow-up** — "I was wrong. Here's what 2 years of data actually showed me."
3. **Numbered promise** — "5 things I stopped doing that doubled my reach:"
4. **Provocative question** — "What if everything you know about personal branding is backwards?"

---

## Post Length

| Length         | When to Use                                | Typical Performance     |
|----------------|--------------------------------------------|-------------------------|
| Short (50-150w) | Hot takes, provocative questions, quotes  | High share rate         |
| Medium (150-300w) | Stories, insights, mini-frameworks      | Best all-around         |
| Long (300-600w) | Deep dives, tutorials, full frameworks    | High save + comment rate |

**Rule:** Match length to value. Don't pad. Don't cut substance to seem "punchy."

---

## Formatting

### Line Breaks
- Use line breaks after every 1-3 sentences
- Never post a wall of text — it signals effort-to-read = scroll past
- Each new paragraph is a new idea or beat

### Lists
- Use when presenting 3+ parallel items
- Start each item with the key word, not a weak introductory word
- Keep list items consistent in length and structure
- Maximum 7 items before a list becomes exhausting

### Bold & Emphasis
- Bold the single most important phrase per section
- Do not bold randomly — it dilutes impact
- Avoid ALL CAPS (reads as shouting)

### Emojis
- Use only when replacing or reinforcing a word
- Maximum 1-2 per post unless the format calls for more (e.g., numbered steps)
- Never use as decorative filler at the start of every line

## Engagement Best Practices

### Commenting Strategy
- Reply to every comment within the first 60 minutes of posting
- Early engagement signals quality to the algorithm — it amplifies reach
- Ask a follow-up question in replies to extend the thread
- Never reply with just "Thanks!" — add value or insight

### First-Hour Rule
After publishing:
1. Spend 30 minutes engaging on OTHER people's posts first (warms the algorithm)
2. Publish
3. Reply to every comment within the first hour
4. Avoid editing the post in the first 24 hours (resets the distribution)

### What to Do With Comments
- Pin the most insightful comment (drives more comments from others)
- If a comment asks a good question, answer it with a mini-post length reply
- Disagreement in comments is good — engage respectfully, don't delete

---

## Posting Cadence

**Minimum viable:** 3x/week
**Optimal:** 4-5x/week
**Maximum:** 7x/week (quality risk increases)

**Time of day (general — validate with own analytics):**
- Best: Tuesday–Thursday, 7:00–9:00 AM or 12:00–1:00 PM (audience timezone)
- Avoid: Weekends (lower organic reach for B2B content)
- Test: Friday morning often outperforms expectations

**Never sacrifice quality for frequency.** One excellent post beats five forgettable ones.

---

## Algorithm Signals (in priority order)

1. **Dwell time** — how long people read before scrolling (long copy helps)
2. **Comments** — weighted higher than likes
3. **Reposts without comment** — lowest weight
4. **"See more" clicks** — hook performance signal
5. **Profile clicks** — strong intent signal
6. **Saves** — strong long-term interest signal

**Implication:** Optimize for comments and dwell time, not likes.

---

## What to Avoid

### Content Anti-Patterns
- Engagement bait ("Comment YES if you agree")
- Humble bragging disguised as a lesson ("Just got featured in Forbes…")
- Vague inspirational quotes with no original perspective added
- Reposting others' content without substantial commentary
- Complaining or venting (even if relatable — damages authority)
- Overly political or divisive topics unless it's your brand's core purpose

### Format Anti-Patterns
- Hashtag spam (3 maximum, relevant only)
- Tagging people who aren't actually referenced in the post
- Posting links in the body (LinkedIn suppresses external links — put them in comments)
- Starting every post with the same structure — vary it

### Behavioral Anti-Patterns
- Ghosting your comments section
- Posting and immediately going offline
- Buying followers or engagement (destroys organic reach long-term)
- Copying competitor posts with minor edits

---

## Links Strategy

LinkedIn suppresses posts with external links in the body.

**Rule:** Never put a link in the post body.

**Instead:**
1. Post without the link
2. In the first comment, write: "Link in comments 👇" — then add the link
3. Or: "Full guide linked in my bio" (drives profile visits too)

---

## Pre-Publish Checklist

- [ ] Hook: Does the first line stop a scroll?
- [ ] Hook: Is it 2 lines or fewer before "see more"?
- [ ] Body: Is every sentence necessary?
- [ ] Format: Are paragraphs short (1-3 lines)?
- [ ] Voice: Does it sound like Celsux, not a generic creator?
- [ ] Value: Can the reader use this immediately?
- [ ] Closer: Is there a question, reflection, or next step?
- [ ] Links: Are external links in comments, not in the body?
- [ ] Hashtags: 3 or fewer, all relevant
- [ ] Tags: Only tagging people who are actually mentioned
